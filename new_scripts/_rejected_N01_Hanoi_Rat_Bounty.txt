In 1902, Hanoi paid people to kill rats, and ended up with more rats than ever.

The French rulers were terrified of plague, and the new sewers under their part of the city were crawling with rats. So they made an offer: one cent for every dead rat.

Hunters poured into the tunnels. Soon, piles of bodies were stacking up outside city hall, so the officials changed one rule. Don't bring the rat. Just bring the tail.

The numbers exploded. Five hundred tails a day. Then eight thousand. Then over twenty thousand. The city was winning.

Until health inspectors visited the villages outside town and spotted something strange: rats running around with no tails.

You see, a rat without a tail can still have babies. So hunters cut off the tail, let the rat go, and waited for it to bring them more. Some villagers even opened rat farms just to grow tails.

The bounty was cancelled, and the rats stayed.

Hanoi paid for dead rats. It got a rat business.
