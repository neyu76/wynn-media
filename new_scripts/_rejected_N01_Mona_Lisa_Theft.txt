The Mona Lisa became the world's most famous painting because a former museum worker walked out of the Louvre with it.

On a Monday morning in 1911, while the museum was closed, Vincenzo Peruggia slipped inside wearing the same white smock as the staff. He lifted the painting off the wall, pulled it out of its frame, wrapped it in his smock, and strolled right back out the door.

Nobody noticed until the next day, when an artist showed up to copy it and found an empty wall.

You see, back then, the Mona Lisa was famous mostly among art lovers. Now she was on every front page. Police even questioned Pablo Picasso. And crowds lined up at the Louvre just to stare at the empty spot where she used to hang.

Meanwhile, she was sitting in a trunk in Peruggia's tiny Paris room. Police visited him twice and never found her.

Two years later, he tried to sell her in Florence and got caught.

But by then, the whole world knew her smile.
