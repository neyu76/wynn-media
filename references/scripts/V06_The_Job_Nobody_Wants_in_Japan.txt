TITLE: The Job Nobody Wants in Japan
NGUỒN: TÁI DỰNG — đọc phụ đề trên hình (lấy 4 khung/giây) + ASR offline. Từ trong [ ] không thấy trên phụ đề, suy từ ngữ pháp/ASR.
MỨC TIN CẬY: bản làm việc, chưa phải bản chép gốc của chủ kênh.

======================================================================
KỊCH BẢN / TRANSCRIPT
======================================================================

It's impossible to get fired in Japan, [so] companies found something way worse. Imagine your boss walks you past your desk to a tiny room with no computer and no work, just a ticking clock and a guy with his feet up, scrolling his phone like he's on vacation. Your new job, your boss says, is nothing. Just sit in here until six. And you're thinking, "Wait, I'm getting paid for this?" At first, it's paradise.

Then your coworkers start staring when you walk past. Your emails stop. And your old desk already has someone else's family photos on it. Every Friday, your boss pops his head in with a big smile to ask if you're enjoying your new role. Still, the guy in the corner [has] been doing this for three years and looks happier than anyone in the building. So if they want to pay you to do nothing, fine. You kick your feet up right next to him.

Then one Monday, you walk in and he's gone. His chair is pushed in, his desk is cleared, and nobody will tell you where he went. You see, Japanese law makes it almost impossible to fire someone, [but] there's no law against making them miserable. So they take away your work and your friends, and just wait, because nobody can do nothing forever.
