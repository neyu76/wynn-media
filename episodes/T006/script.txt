This chess machine got the applause. Its hidden player did the thinking.

Known as the Mechanical Turk, it presented a figure seated at a chessboard, with a cabinet underneath. From the outside, the figure appeared to play the game by itself.

Imagine an opponent moving a piece, then leaning forward as the wooden hand answers. The interesting question is not how clever that hand looks. It is who decided where it should go.

Inside the cabinet, a concealed human player supplied the chess skill. Magnetic mechanisms helped the operator follow moves on the board above, while mechanical controls operated the figure's arm.

So the impressive movement was real. The impression of an independent mechanical mind was the trick. A human was making decisions behind a very convincing piece of furniture.

Picture the final bow: the opponent studies the board, the crowd watches the figure, and the player remains out of sight. The part doing the thinking is also the part nobody came to applaud.

Human intelligence. Wooden management.
