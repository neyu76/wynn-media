This customer complained on clay, and the complaint is still here.

The message was from Nanni to a copper dealer named Ea-nasir. According to Nanni's complaint, the copper offered was not the quality he had been promised. He wanted the seller to hear about it.

Imagine expecting good copper and being presented with something you consider unacceptable. Our customer looks from the goods to the promise, then reaches for a writing surface. This time, that surface is clay.

No star rating. No comment box. Just a complaint pressed into clay, carrying his account of a disappointing deal far beyond the original exchange.

What Nanni wanted for future deliveries was more control: he would select the copper ingots himself. In our illustration, the hand that had pointed at the complaint now points toward the goods.

Then the delivery scene fades, while the tablet stays. That ancient complaint survives in the British Museum's collection. A disagreement about copper has left us a remarkably durable record of an unhappy customer.

Customer service. Permanent record.
