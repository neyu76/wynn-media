In this made-up town, the mayor charged people to sit in the shade.

He divided the shade from a town shelter into paid spaces. His sign had a second rule: shade from privately owned awnings was outside the scheme. At first, nobody had one.

As the afternoon passed, the shadow shifted. People shuffled their chairs to stay cool, while the mayor kept pointing at his painted spaces. A lemonade seller watched customers chase the shade instead of visiting her stall.

Nearby, the town was selling an old awning, complete with its folded frame and rolled fabric. The mayor thought repairing it was not worth the trouble. The seller bought it and kept the receipt.

With a little help, she repaired the frame and opened the awning over her tables. Now there was private shade beside the lemonade. People moved their chairs over, and some bought drinks.

The mayor arrived with his price sign. She showed him his own rule, then the receipt. He looked at his empty paid spaces, put the sign down, and ordered a lemonade.

Shade included. Lesson extra.
