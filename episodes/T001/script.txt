He sent bank bricks through the mail because freight cost too much.
In nineteen sixteen, a bank director in Vernal, Utah, needed special bricks for the building's front. Getting those bricks into town was expensive. Sending them as parcels was cheaper.
The postal price followed delivery zones. The actual journey followed difficult terrain. A place that looked close on the map could require a much longer trip.
So the bricks became packages. Ten to a wooden crate, keeping each crate within the fifty pound limit. According to the Postal Service's history, fifteen thousand bricks went through the mail.
Picture the clerk expecting another parcel, then discovering the next parcel is also bricks. And the next. Eventually, those deliveries became the bank's facade.
That November, the rules were clarified: unusually heavy daily shipments from one sender to one recipient needed further instructions before acceptance. The bank's shipment had helped force the issue.
Special delivery. Very special.
