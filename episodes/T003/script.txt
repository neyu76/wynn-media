A newspaper put bat people on the Moon. The discovery was invented.

In eighteen thirty-five, The Sun began a series of six articles describing supposed lunar discoveries. The reports attached those discoveries to the astronomer John Herschel, making the story look connected to real science.

Among the paper's claimed inhabitants were beings with batlike wings. Picture a reader opening the page and finding a whole strange world, delivered with the confidence of a scientific report.

But a scientist's name printed above an extraordinary claim does not make the claim true. Herschel had not discovered these creatures. The animals in our scene belong to the newspaper's invented Moon.

Now follow one of those paper creatures backward. Its wings fold into an illustration, the illustration flattens into a page, and the page slides toward a printing press.

Richard Adams Locke acknowledged writing the hoax. Letters from Herschel's family also record reactions to the fabrication. The name borrowed for credibility and the person behind the story belonged in different files.

Moon creatures. Earthly printing.
