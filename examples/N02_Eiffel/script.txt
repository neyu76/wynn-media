A man sold the Eiffel Tower, and the buyer was so embarrassed, he let him do it again.

In 1925, Paris newspapers said the tower was rusting and costing a fortune. So Victor Lustig printed fake government letterhead, booked a fancy hotel suite, and invited five scrap dealers to a secret meeting. "The tower is coming down," he whispered. "Seven thousand tons of metal. Highest bidder wins."

One dealer, André Poisson, wanted it badly. But something felt off. Then Lustig sighed and said a government salary is tiny, and a gentleman has expenses.

You see, that's what sold it. A con man would never ask for a bribe. Only a real official would. Poisson paid for the tower, paid the bribe, and Lustig left for Austria with the cash.

Then he waited for the police. Nothing. Poisson was too ashamed to tell anyone he'd bought the Eiffel Tower.

So six months later, Lustig came back and sold it again.

The Eiffel Tower was never for sale. It just got sold twice.
